## §1 General

### §1.1 Purpose
This project aims to maintain a respectful, safe, and productive environment for all contributors.

### §1.2 Scope
Every section and subsection of this Code of Conduct is equally binding, regardless of how it's formatted or numbered. Headings are for navigation only and don't limit or change the text beneath them.

### §1.3 Changes
Maintainers may change this Code of Conduct. Every section that existed on the day the first contributor joined the project (not yet) has been in effect since that day. A section added after that day shows the date it was added, in the form *(Added YYYY-MM-DD)*, and a section whose meaning is changed shows *(Amended YYYY-MM-DD: a one-line summary of the change)*. A new or amended section takes effect on the date shown and applies only to behavior on or after that date. Earlier behavior is judged under the rules that were in effect when it happened. No amendment may exempt any person nor role, including a maintainer, from a section of this Code of Conduct that applies to contributors nor may any amendment lessen the steps in [§7.5](#75-maintainer-violations) for maintainers without a public explanation of the reason.

### §1.4 Notation
In this Code of Conduct, letters in parentheses attached to a word without a space mean that either form applies. "Pattern(s)" means one or more patterns, and "a(n)" means "a" or "an." Parentheses that are preceded by a space are ordinary punctuation, and what they contain is part of the text. A forward slash (/) between words means "or" unless the sentence says otherwise. Please make sure to read this Code of Conduct slowly enough to understand the details and not skim it.

## §2 Expected Behavior

### §2.1 Respect
Contributors must treat others with courtesy. Disagreements are allowed, but disrespect, hostility, or dismissive behavior isn't.

### §2.2 Constructive Feedback
Feedback should focus on improving the project. Criticism must be actionable, specific, and delivered without insults or sarcasm.

### §2.3 Follow Project Rules
Contributors must follow maintainer instructions, repository guidelines, and the steps in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). Ignoring rules disrupts development.

### §2.4 Stay On Topic
Discussions should remain relevant to the project. Off-topic debates, personal tangents, or unrelated arguments slow progress.

### §2.5 Inclusive Language
Contributors should use language that is welcoming and professional and should avoid slurs, exclusionary phrasing, or intentionally provocative wording.

## §3 Unacceptable Behavior

### §3.1 Harassment
Harassment includes insults, personal attacks, repeated unwanted contact, publishing someone's private information (doxxing), or attempts to provoke emotional distress.

### §3.2 Threats or Intimidation
Any form of threat—technical, personal, or social—is prohibited. This includes coercion, blackmail, or fear-based pressure.

### §3.3 Hate Speech
Discriminatory language targeting race, gender, sexuality, nationality, disability, religion, or personal beliefs are strictly forbidden.

### §3.4 Leaking Exploits
Posting harmful code, vulnerabilities, or exploit details publicly isn't allowed. Use GitHub’s private reporting tools instead.

### §3.5 Spam
Spam includes irrelevant messages, repeated posts, or attempts to derail conversations. 

### §3.6 Trolling
Trolling includes baiting, antagonizing, or intentionally causing conflict.

### §3.7 Impersonation
Pretending to be another contributor or maintainer is prohibited.

### §3.8 Sockpuppeting
Creating new accounts to bypass moderation is prohibited.

### §3.9 Disruption
Actions intended to sabotage development—such as derailing issues, spreading misinformation, or intentionally breaking workflows—are not allowed.

### §3.10 Rewording and Workarounds
This Code of Conduct applies to the meaning and effect of what is said / done, not only to the exact words used. Doing (or not doing) anything that any section of this Code of Conduct requires, prohibits, or limits is treated the same whether done directly or indirectly.

#### §3.10.1 Rewording
Synonyms, euphemism (softer or indirect wording), coded language, misspellings, symbols, images, sarcasm, or "just joking" framing are treated the same as the direct version of what they say.

#### §3.10.2 Splitting
Dividing an act across several messages, posts, or accounts is treated as a single act. Maintainers consider the messages together, including the order they were posted and any pattern(s) between them, even if no single message would violate this Code of Conduct on its own.

#### §3.10.3 Acting Through Others
Getting another person to act on someone's behalf, or encouraging or coordinating others to do something prohibited, is treated as doing it directly.

#### §3.10.4 Tampering with Evidence
Altering, deleting, or fabricating content or evidence in a report, an appeal, or the project history is treated as a violation of this Code of Conduct.

#### §3.10.5 Interpretation
Maintainers decide whether behavior falls under §3.10 by considering what a reasonable reader would understand it to mean and the context in which it appeared. A violation under §3.10 is treated as the same severity as the act it disguises. Intent to follow the letter of a rule while ignoring its purpose doesn't excuse a violation.

### §3.11 Evading Enforcement
Evading or trying to evade an action taken under this Code of Conduct is prohibited. This includes:

* Returning through another account after a block, or continuing behavior that was warned about (This means a violation of §3.8 is a violation of §3.11)
* Repeating removed content or continuing a prohibited behavior in another place such as a new issue, a discussion, or a fork
* Timing or spacing violations to take advantage of a record reset

Evasion is a violation in itself, and maintainers may treat it as a repeat violation. A record reset doesn't apply to a person who evaded enforcement. See [§4.6 (Limits on Record Resets)](#46-limits-on-record-resets) for limits on resets.

### §3.12 Closing and Reopening Issues
A maintainer may close an issue in either of the cases below. Closing is a review decision under §7.4 and is not an action under this Code of Conduct.

* **Invalid:** a required field in the issue's template is empty or filled with text that does not answer it, such as "n/a." The maintainer labels the issue "invalid." Fields that are not required may be left empty.
* **Duplicate:** another issue already covers the same problem or request. The maintainer labels the issue "duplicate" and links to the original issue in the closing comment. A person with more information can add it to the original issue.

The closing comment cites this section. A person who thinks the decision was wrong may say why in a comment on the closed issue instead of reopening it, and the maintainer reads the comment and may reopen the issue. Reports of security vulnerabilities and accessibility problems are not closed as invalid. A duplicate of one of them is closed only after the maintainer replies and links to the original.

Reopening an issue closed this way, without changing it to fix the reason it was closed, is a violation of §3.9 The first time, the maintainer closes it again with a comment that cites this section. Repeating it after that comment is handled under §6.2.

## §4 Enforcement
Maintainers may take any of the following actions in §4.1 to §4.5:

### §4.1 Issue a warning
A maintainer may privately or publicly warn a contributor whose behavior violates this Code of Conduct.

### §4.2 Hide or delete harmful content
Comments, issues, pull requests, or other content that is harmful or abusive may be hidden / deleted.

### §4.3 Block the user from the repository
A user who continues to violate this Code of Conduct may be blocked from the repository. For specific numbers about blocking, see [§6.2 (Temporary or Permanent Blocking)](#62-temporary-or-permanent-blocking).

### §4.4 Report violations to GitHub Trust & Safety
Serious violations may be [reported to GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

### §4.5 Remove contributions that violate this Code of Conduct
Contributions that violate this Code of Conduct may be removed.

### §4.6 Limits on record resets
A record reset under §6.5 clears warnings and temporary blocks from the count in §6.2, but it does not erase the history. Maintainers may take a reset into account in the following cases:

* **Pattern of behavior:** repeating a violation shortly after a reset, or spacing violations so that each falls in a different year, may be treated as a repeat violation instead of a first one.
* **Severe violations:** records of severe violations, uh-oh blocks, and permanent blocks are never reset.
* **Evasion:** a reset does not apply to a person who evaded enforcement under §3.11, such as by using another account during a block.

Maintainers decide whether a pattern exists and may apply any action in §4.1 to §4.5.

## §5 Reporting
To report violations:

### §5.1 Report abuse on GitHub
Use GitHub’s “Report abuse” feature on the user’s profile.

### §5.2 Report to the maintainers
Where available, use “Report to repository admins” on the content. Otherwise, open the repository’s **Security and quality** tab, click **Report a vulnerability**, and begin the title with “Code of Conduct report”.

Include:

* The section(s) of this Code of Conduct that you believe were violated (for example, §2.1 if you beleive the violator was disrespectful)
* Links or screenshots of what happened
* The date it occurred
* A short description of the incident

Reports will be handled confidentially.

## §6 Consequences
Violations may result in:

### §6.1 Content removal
Content that violates this Code of Conduct may be removed.

### §6.2 Temporary or permanent blocking
Blocking is applied in steps, depending on severity and history:

* **1<sup>st</sup> violation:** a warning, or a temporary block of up to 7 days.
* **2<sup>nd</sup> violation:** a temporary block of 30 days.
* **3<sup>rd</sup> violation:** a serious block.

Severe violations are those under §3.1 to §3.4, and any other violation maintainers judge to cause serious harm. These may skip the steps above and result in 2 serious blocks.

A **serious block** is a block of 365 days. A violation by a person returning from a serious block, within the period in §6.5, results in another serious block. The first four serious blocks may be appealed after they end, under §6.6.1. The 5<sup>th</sup> serious block is called the "uh-oh". When an uh-oh ends, it is reviewed under §6.6.7, and the review ends in either reinstatement or a permanent block. A permanent block is issued only through that review.

A serious block that is overturned on appeal does not count toward the 5. An uh-oh isn't issued while an appeal of an earlier serious block is pending or the 14-day period to file one is open.

### §6.3 Escalation to GitHub Trust & Safety
Serious violations may be [escalated to GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

### §6.4 Removal of contributions
Contributions that violate this Code of Conduct may be removed.

### §6.5 Record keeping
Maintainers keep private records of Code of Conduct violations, including the date, what happened, and the action taken. These records are used to apply the steps in §6.2 consistently. They are not published, and they are shared only with other maintainers, GitHub, or as required by law.

#### §6.5.1 Reset Period
The 365 days are counted from the date the most recent violation occurred, or from the date a block ends, whichever is later. If maintainers learn of it later, the date it occurred still controls, not the date it was found.

#### §6.5.2 Cleaning A Record
A person's earlier warnings and temporary blocks are reset only after 365 days with **NO NEW VIOLATION**. A new violation restarts the 365 days for the whole record, and all earlier violations count toward the steps in §6.2 until a full year passes without another.

#### §6.5.3 Exceptions
Records of severe violations, uh-oh blocks, serious blocks, and permanent blocks are never reset. See [§4.6](#46-limits-on-record-resets) for limits on resets.

### §6.6 Appeals
A person who receives a warning, content removal, or block under this Code of Conduct may ask the maintainer to reconsider it.

#### §6.6.1 How to appeal
Appeals must be made within 14 days of the action, by replying where the action was announced (for example, in the same issue or pull request) or through the channel in [§5.2](#52-report-to-the-maintainers). For a block, the 14 days start on the day the block ends. An appeal should name the section(s) involved and explain why the action was mistaken / too severe.

#### §6.6.2 Review
This project has one maintainer, so appeals are reviewed by the same person who made the decision. The maintainer waits at least 14 days before deciding and rereads the original record. The maintainer may uphold, reduce, or overturn the action, and the decision is final.

#### §6.6.3 Effect on records
An overturned action is removed from the record kept under §6.5 and doesn't count toward the steps in §6.2. Filing an appeal doesn't pause the action. **ONE** appeal is allowed per action, and repeated or abusive appeals may be treated as a violation of §3.9 (Disruption).

#### §6.6.4 Blocked users
A blocked user cannot contact the project through GitHub. A person who has been temporarily blocked, or given one of the first four serious blocks, may appeal after the block ends. An uh-oh is reviewed under §6.6.7. A permanent block cannot be appealed.

#### §6.6.5 Reports about the maintainer
Because the maintainer is the only reviewer, concerns about the maintainer's own conduct can be reported to GitHub under [§5.1 (Report Abuse on Github)](#51-report-abuse-on-github). GitHub applies its own rules to such reports, not this Code of Conduct.

#### §6.6.6 Abusive appeals
A denied appeal isn't automatically a violation. An appeal may be treated as a violation of this section (and potentially others) only if it includes insults, includes threats (§3.2), includes harassment (§3.1), repeats an argument after a final decision (§3.9), is filed through an account other than the one that received the action, or relies on fabricated / altered evidence (§3.10.4). The maintainer may close such an appeal without further response.

#### §6.6.7 Review of the uh-oh
This section applies to an uh-oh in place of §6.6.1 to §6.6.3. When the 365 days of an uh-oh end, the blocked account is unblocked for the sole purpose of appealing.

##### §6.6.7.1 Window
The window opens at 19:00 CT on the day the block ends and closes at 19:00 CT the next day. The notice of the block states both times and links to the appeal schedule at [ENFORCEMENT.md](ENFORCEMENT.md#appeal-windows). If the account is unblocked later than the opening time, the closing time moves later by the same amount.

##### §6.6.7.2 Postponement
If the maintainer is unavailable, the maintainer may move the window to a later day, by up to 7 days. The new opening and closing times are posted in the appeal schedule, without names, before the original opening time. If the maintainer cannot post before then, the window opens when the maintainer posts and lasts 24 hours. A window is never moved earlier. A postponement is not counted against the person, and the 365 days in §6.5.1 are counted from the original end date of the block.

##### §6.6.7.3 How to appeal
One issue titled "Appeal" opened in this repository. The account posts **NOTHING** else during the window.

##### §6.6.7.4 Other content
Anything else the account posts during the window is hidden, and a private copy is kept under §6.5. The copy may be used in the review only if it violates this Code of Conduct or contradicts the appeal.

##### §6.6.7.5 Decision
The account is blocked again when the window closes. The maintainer waits at least 14 days, rereads the record, and either lifts the uh-oh and lets the person back in, or issues a permanent block. The decision is final.

##### §6.6.7.6 If no appeal is filed
If no appeal is filed, a permanent block is issued.

##### §6.6.7.7 If lifted
The record stays, and any new violation results in a permanent block without the earlier steps.

##### §6.6.7.8 Counts
By January 31 of each year, the maintainer publishes, for the previous calendar year, the number of uh-oh blocks issued, uh-oh reviews decided, and uh-oh blocks lifted, without names, at [ENFORCEMENT.md](ENFORCEMENT.md#counts).

##### §6.6.7.9 Misuse of the appeal form
The appeal form, and any issue titled "Appeal," are only for an uh-oh appeal made during its window by the account that received the uh-oh. Using them for anything else is misuse. This includes opening one from an account that did not receive an uh-oh, opening one on behalf of another person, opening more than one in a window, and giving an issue the title "Appeal" for a different purpose.

An issue opened by mistake is closed with a comment that cites this section and says how to open the right kind of issue. That is not a violation. Misuse done on purpose, or repeated after that comment, is a violation of [§3.9](#39-disruption) and is handled under [§6.2](#62-temporary-or-permanent-blocking). An appeal made by the account that received the uh-oh, within its window, is never closed under this section, even if it is short or badly written.

## §7 Maintainer Accountability
This Code of Conduct applies to maintainers in the same way it applies to everyone else. Maintainers hold themselves to the standards in §2 and §3 when they review contributions, answer issues, and enforce this document.

### §7.1 Pause before enforcing
When a maintainer is personally involved in a dispute, e.g. as the target of an insult, they wait at least 24 hours before taking any action under §4, other than hiding content that is an immediate threat or an exposure of private information. In a dispute where the maintainer is personally involved, a warning under §4.1 is preferred over a block, unless the violation is severe according to §6.2.

### §7.2 Cite the rule
Every enforcement action names the section(s) that were violated and links to or screenshots of the content involved. Maintainers don't act on a general impression of someone's attitude.

### §7.3 No Favoritism
Maintainers apply the steps in §6.2, the record rules in §6.5, and the appeal rules in §6.6 in the same way regardless of who is involved, including friends, frequent contributors, themselves, and critics.

### §7.4 No misuse of enforcement
Enforcement tools can't be used to end technical disagreements, silence polite criticism of the project or its maintainer, or settle personal conflicts. Declining a contribution on technical grounds is a review decision and isn't an action under this Code of Conduct.

### §7.5 Maintainer violations
If a maintainer violates this Code of Conduct, the steps below apply.

#### §7.5.1 Correction
Within 7 days of becoming aware of the violation, the maintainer edits / removes the content and publicly acknowledges the problem, naming the section violated. Content is edited with a visible note and isn't silently deleted, in line with §3.10.4.

#### §7.5.2 Records
Maintainer violations are recorded in the same private records as other violations under §6.5, with the same details and the same 365-day period.

#### §7.5.3 Repeat violations
Maintainer violations are counted using the records and the 365-day period in §6.5, the same way as for contributors.

* **2<sup>nd</sup> violation:** the maintainer takes no action under §4.1 to §4.5 for 30 days, except hiding content that is an immediate threat or an exposure of private information, and publishes a short note saying which section was violated and what will change.
* **3<sup>rd</sup> violation:** the maintainer permanently stops enforcement under §4.1 to §4.5, so conduct reports go to GitHub only; publishes a statement listing the sections violated and the dates; and releases only bug fixes and security fixes for 90 days.
* **4<sup>th</sup> violation:** the maintainer publishes a final notice listing the section(s) violated and the dates, continues to release security fixes for 90 days, and then publishes a final release that works as before and states that the project is no longer maintained. The maintainer then archives the repository, does not unarchive it, and does not restart or continue the project, or a substantially similar one, under any name.

#### §7.5.4 Severe violations
A violation by a maintainer under §3.1 to §3.4 may skip the steps in §7.5.3, in the same way as §6.2 for contributors.

#### §7.5.5 Reports to GitHub
A violation that the maintainer doesn't correct can be reported to GitHub under [§5.1 (Report abuse on GitHub)](#51-report-abuse-on-github), as described in §6.6.5. GitHub applies its own rules to the report and not this Code of Conduct.

### §7.6 Accusations Against Maintainers
A claim that a maintainer violated this Code of Conduct or failed to follow §7 should include:

* The section(s) said to be violated
* Links or screenshots of the content involved
* The date it occurred

A claim without these may be closed with a request for the missing details. A claim that is denied isn't automatically a violation. A claim that relies on fabricated or altered evidence or on content that is shown not to exist is treated as a violation of §3.10.4. Claims repeated after a final answer may be treated as a violation of §3.9, and §6.6.6 applies to them in the same way.
